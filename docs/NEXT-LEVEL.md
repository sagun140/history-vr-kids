# Taking Butterfly Atlas to the next level

This document is the closing report of a 100-agent idea swarm run against the repository as it stands today: 189 events, 209 causal links, an AP World History: Modern test kit and a WebXR build both in flight, and no build step anywhere. The swarm produced 240 raw ideas, which scoring reduced to 141 that survived on impact, cost and fit with what this project actually is. What follows is the ten strongest bets overall, then the full plan in three tracks — AP and assessment, VR and immersion, and content, platform and reach — each with what to do in days, in weeks, in months, and what we are deliberately not doing.

Every idea here respects the same four constraints: free and open, double-click-a-file wherever possible, truthful and honest about historical debate, and usable by a kid with no account.

## The ten bets

1. **A wrong answer becomes a broken thread you go repair.** `atlasLinks()` in ap.js today opens `index.html?event=id`, which just lights up a dot and ends — but every question already names its atlas events in `q(..., atlas)`, so a missed item can instead drop the student into hide-the-middle on the exact chain the distractor caught them missing. This is the one thing a prep book structurally cannot do, and it is the difference between a predicted score and a diagnostic. *First step:* extend `readURL()` in app.js to accept `hide=1` and `focus=<id>` alongside the existing `then`/`now`/`era` params, then build that URL in `renderReport()`'s missed loop in ap.js.

2. **A way in that isn't a 4.2px circle.** The only entry point to all 189 events is an SVG `<circle class="ev" tabindex="0">` with no `aria-label`, so a screen reader says "clickable" 189 times and a keyboard user tabs forever; a combobox above the map, plus labelled dots, plus one dedicated live region so the panel stops re-reading the whole chain on every click, flips the app from unusable to usable end to end. Nothing else on this list changes who can open the door. *First step:* add `<input id="eventSearch" role="combobox">` and a `<ul role="listbox">` to the `.stage` section of index.html, wire them to the existing `openEvent(id)` in app.js, and set an `aria-label` in the `el("circle", …)` call.

3. **Put the VR HUD inside the scene.** `#hud` is a `position:fixed` DOM div, which a headset never composites, so every line `sayHud()` writes — "Then moved to 1492", "Pick two moments that are linked first" — is visible on a laptop and invisible in VR, precisely where the user has no other feedback. Until this lands, the VR build is a demo that silently withholds its own instructions. *First step:* in vr.js, have `sayHud()` retexture a small billboard plane parented to `#rig` in vr.html as well as writing the DOM div.

4. **Show the guesses.** Sixty-six of 189 events carry `ca:true` and nothing on screen says so beyond a "c." prefix; one toggle that recolours every approximate dot and arc amber turns the entire deep-past half of the map over at once. It is the fastest lesson in "before writing, history is reconstruction, not record" this app can deliver, and it needs no new data. *First step:* add a `.ev.guess` dotted-amber rule to the `<style>` block in index.html and a toggle in `.presets` that sets the class from `byId.get(c.dataset.id).ca` inside `render()` in app.js.

5. **Make VR spacing mean years.** `buildCorridor()` uses a flat `GAP = 1.9` and bows each arc by screen distance, so the 1,300 years from Han paper to Gutenberg is the same walk as the three years from Tambora to Frankenstein. Making station spacing and arc height proportional to the real year gap is the entire argument for the headset: a causal thread gets a length you feel in your legs. *First step:* in vr.js's `buildCorridor()`, compute a per-link gap (clamped roughly 1.2–6 m) from `byId.get(b).y - byId.get(a).y` instead of the constant `GAP`.

6. **"Show me the sentence" self-scoring.** Self-scored SAQ/DBQ/LEQ points in `rubricHTML()` are a bare 0/1 button, which every student on earth inflates; requiring the student to paste the sentence from their own response that earns each point, and printing those quotes on the report, makes the score honest and auditable in ten seconds. It also teaches the rubric, which the button never did. *First step:* add a small `<input data-cite="dbq:0">` per row in `rubricHTML()` in ap.js and store it beside `S.dbqSelf` in the state object.

7. **"+N other causes" on every chain link.** Fifty-two events have more than one incoming link and one has six, but `renderBridge` draws only the edge that happens to lie on the traced path — which is exactly the single-thread-of-destiny story the app should be undercutting. A badge that expands the other real parents turns the signature move into an honest one, from data already on disk. *First step:* add `otherCauses(to, usedFrom) => inn.get(to).filter(l => l.from !== usedFrom)` beside `whyOf` in threads.js and splice a badge into the `.why` paragraph template in app.js.

8. **Distractor Forge: graph-generated question drafts with a human gate.** Distractor quality is what separates a real item from a fake one, and the atlas already knows which events are same-`kind`, near in year and *not* linked — the exact shape of a plausible wrong answer. Drafts land in a review file a human promotes by hand, so nothing auto-publishes and the honesty note at the top of apdata.js stays true. *First step:* write `tools/forge.mjs` (sibling to check.mjs) that returns same-kind / no-edge / within-150-years candidates for a given event and emits ready-to-paste `q(skill, text, choices, answer, why, atlas)` drafts.

9. **Grab a thread and read it; pull it and break it.** The tubes in `threadGroup` aren't raycastable at all today — only the instanced pins are — so the most physical object in the room is the one thing you cannot touch. Making links pickable, showing `whyOf()` on pinch, and letting a hard pull snap a link so every downstream station goes dark is the counterfactual lesson the flat atlas gets for free from the same change. *First step:* change `findChains(state, limit)` in threads.js to `findChains(state, limit, banned = new Set())` with one `banned.has(nx.id)` guard, then make the tubes raycast targets in vr.js.

10. **`contribute.html`: authoring without git.** The only way to add history today is hand-editing a 422-line file, which caps the dataset at whatever one maintainer types; a static page that loads data.js, takes lat/lon from a map click, and emits a validated, copy-pasteable `ev(...)`/`ln(...)` line is the unlock from 189 events to thousands. The validation has to be the same code CI runs, or the gate is theatre. *First step:* extract the id/time/kind checks from check.mjs into a `validate.js` callable from both node and the browser, and prove `node check.mjs` still passes unchanged.

## AP & assessment track

The kit already does what a prep book does — 55 stimulus MCQs across 19 sets, three SAQs, a seven-document DBQ, three LEQs, real per-section clocks, weighted scoring, a predicted 1–5, and per-unit and per-skill breakdowns. A book cannot do the two things this repo is uniquely positioned for: every question already names its atlas events in `q(..., atlas)`, and data.js holds a 209-edge causal graph. The bet is that the exam stops being a score and becomes a diagnostic that hands the student a specific broken causal link and a way back into the map to repair it.

### Now (days)

- **Missed question → repair the thread.** `atlasLinks()` (ap.js:141) currently opens `index.html?event=id` — a dead end that just shows the dot. Make the score report's "what to look at again" block emit a chain link instead: two atlas ids from the missed question become `?then=…&now=…&hide=1`, dropping the student into hide-the-middle on the exact causal chain the distractor caught them missing. *First step: extend `readURL()` in app.js (line 113) to accept `hide=1` and `focus=<id>` alongside the existing `then`/`now`/`era` params, then build that URL in `renderReport()`'s missed loop in ap.js.*
- **Real DBQ reading-period lockout.** ap.js:225 already computes whether the clock is inside the 15-minute reading window but only prints "suggested reading period" — the textarea stays live. Disable it, swap in a pencils-down banner and a documents-only layout, then unlock at the flip. *First step: in ap.js's `renderDBQ`, gate the `<textarea data-dbq>` on the same `left > (SEC.dbq.minutes - SEC.dbq.reading)*60` expression that already drives the label.*
- **"Show me the sentence" self-scoring.** Self-scored SAQ/DBQ/LEQ points (`rubricHTML`, ap.js:291) are currently a bare 0/1 button, which every student inflates. Require the student to paste the sentence from their own response that earns each point before the point counts, and print those quotes on the report so a teacher can audit in ten seconds. *First step: add a small `<input data-cite="dbq:0">` per row in `rubricHTML()` and store it next to `S.dbqSelf` in the state object at ap.js:26.*
- **Blueprint audit in apcheck.mjs.** The real exam weights units and skills to published percentages; nothing currently checks that the 55 questions match. Add a non-fatal report of MCQ counts per unit and per `skill` against target bands, so a contributor adding questions can see the bank drifting toward Units 5–7. *First step: after the existing checks in apcheck.mjs, tally `MCQ.flatMap(s => s.qs)` by `s.unit` and `q.skill` and print a table with target ranges.*
- **Don't lose a three-hour run to a stray click.** A 3h15 timed attempt dying on a reload or a closed tab is the worst possible failure. Guard `beforeunload` while a section is live and checkpoint answers to localStorage on every change, so a reopened tab offers to resume the attempt in progress. (Offline survival itself is the shared service worker in the platform track — this is only the in-tab half.) *First step: add a `window.onbeforeunload` guard in ap.js gated on `timed()`, plus a `saveRun()`/`resumeRun()` pair writing `S` to localStorage.*

### Next (weeks)

- **Distractor Forge: graph-generated question drafts with a human gate.** Distractor quality is what separates real items from fake ones; the atlas already knows which events are same-`kind`, near in year, and *not* linked — exactly the shape of a plausible wrong answer. Generate draft `q(...)` rows into a review file that a human edits and promotes into apdata.js; nothing auto-publishes, so the honesty note at the top of apdata.js stays true. *First step: write `tools/forge.mjs` (sibling to check.mjs) that for a given event returns same-kind / no-edge / within-150-years candidates and emits ready-to-paste `q(skill, text, choices, answer, why, atlas)` drafts.*
- **Push back on my thesis (no LLM).** A button beside the LEQ/DBQ textarea that fuzzy-matches the typed thesis against `EVENTS` titles, finds the real sibling causes in `LINKS` the student did not mention, and escalates over three clicks from a vague nudge to naming the specific counter-event by title and year. Every prompt is templated from checked graph data, so it cannot invent history or write the essay. *First step: add `weakestClaim(text)` to ap.js using the same `out`/`inn` adjacency build as `findChains()` in threads.js.*
- **Link-level mastery, not card-level.** Log every missed MCQ down to the `from>to` link ids behind its `atlas` array, and schedule those links on SM-2 intervals in localStorage. "Review 6 links you missed" then sets `state.a`/`state.b` via `toPos()` and replays them in hide-the-middle. *First step: create `srs.js` with a pure `nextInterval(quality, reps, ease)` and a store keyed by `from>to`, seeded from `q.atlas` in ap.js's scoring path.*
- **Paper mode: bubble sheet plus a grader screen.** One Chromebook and thirty kids is the normal case. Extend `printSheet()` (ap.js:365) with a numbered answer grid, and add a teacher screen where typing 55 letters produces the same per-unit and per-skill meters and repair links as the on-screen report. *First step: add `data-print="bubbles"` to the printSheet switch and a `renderGrade()` view reusing `renderReport()`'s `byUnit`/`bySkill` tally.*
- **Zero-PII class results.** A student finishes, gets a short base64 blob (or QR) of answer letters and self-scores; the teacher's browser decodes many of them into one class table. No server, no accounts, nothing leaves the device — structurally exempt from a student-data agreement, which is the actual adoption gate. *First step: write `classcode.js` with `encodeRun(S)` / `decodeRun(str)` over `S.order`/`S.ans`/`S.*Self`, wired to a new chip beside "Score report" in ap.js's home tiles.*

### Later (months)

- **A causation report card the College Board does not print.** Alongside unit and skill meters, report performance by atlas `kind` (trade/science/power/…) and by causal distance — whether the student fails on one-hop links or only on four-hop chains. That is a diagnostic no prep book can compute.
- **Form B, and a bank that keeps growing.** A second full 55-question form plus a second DBQ document set, built through the Forge pipeline so the marginal cost of form three is review time, not authoring time.
- **The same engine, other syllabi.** A `curricula.js` lens that relabels units and skills and swaps command-term stems (IB "evaluate the extent", GCSE "explain one way") over the identical question bank and rubric machinery.
- **Exam-kit accessibility pass.** Timed sections plus `innerHTML` re-renders are the worst case for screen readers; a dedicated one-line live region for clock warnings, question position and right/wrong feedback, verified in smoke.mjs.

### Not doing

- **LLM auto-grading of DBQ/LEQ essays.** It needs an API key, ships a kid's writing off-device, breaks double-click-to-open, and hallucinates history — the rubric-plus-cite-your-sentence flow teaches more and costs nothing.
- **Accounts, a hosted gradebook, or class leaderboards.** The moment student work touches a server this becomes a FERPA/COPPA procurement item and a single teacher can no longer just try it; URL blobs and printouts cover the real need.
- **Reproducing actual released College Board items or documents.** The honesty note at the top of apdata.js is a feature, not a limitation — original paraphrases keep the kit legally and pedagogically clean.
- **Tightening the predicted score past a 1–5 band.** The real curve is set annually and unpublished; a decimal-point prediction would be confident fiction.

## VR & immersion track

The flat atlas can show you a chain; only the headset can make you *cross* one. The bet is that VR's whole advantage here is that a causal thread gets a body — a length you walk, a height you look up at, a gap between two events you feel in your legs — so every hour of this track goes into making time physical and honest, not into scenery. vr.js already stands you at the table, arcs the threads, and turns a chain into a corridor; what follows is making that room tell the truth, survive a class period on a Quest, and eventually hold two people at once.

### Now (days)

- **Put the HUD inside the scene.** `#hud` is a `position:fixed` DOM div, which the headset never composites — so every `sayHud()` line ("Then moved to 1492", "Pick two moments that are linked first") is visible on a laptop and invisible in VR, exactly where the user has no other feedback. *First step: in vr.js, have `sayHud()` retexture a small billboard plane parented to `#rig` in vr.html as well as writing the DOM div.*
- **Stop leaking textures while a kid drags the timeline.** `drawThreads()` calls `this.labelGroup.clear()`, which detaches the node labels without disposing their canvas textures; each `recompute()` allocates up to nine fresh 512×128 `CanvasTexture`s. On a desktop you never notice; on a Quest, ten minutes of dragging is a hard stall. *First step: in vr.js's `drawThreads()`, replace `this.labelGroup.clear()` with a loop that disposes each child's `material.map`, `material` and `geometry` first (the thread tubes already do this).*
- **One link, laptop or headset.** app.js has `writeURL()`/`readURL()` reading `?then=&now=&era=&event=`; vr.js ignores all of it, so a teacher's shared link silently drops you at paper→Moon in VR. *First step: in vr.js's `init()`, seed `this.state.a/.b/.w` from `new URLSearchParams(location.search)` through `toPos()`, mirroring `readURL()` in app.js, and write the same params back on `recompute()`.*
- **Spacing that means years.** `buildCorridor()` uses a flat `GAP = 1.9` and `drawThreads()` bows each arc by *screen distance*, so 1,300 years from Han paper to Gutenberg is the same walk as three years from Tambora to Frankenstein. Make station spacing (clamped, say 1.2–6 m) and arc height proportional to `byId.get(b).y - byId.get(a).y`. *First step: in vr.js's `buildCorridor()`, compute a per-link gap from the chain's own years instead of the constant `GAP`.*
- **Thumbstick scrub, never a walk.** Bind right-stick X to `state.b` and left-stick X to `state.a` so the app's real verb — dragging two eras — is native in VR, with no locomotion rig and no optic flow. *First step: in vr.js's `tick()`, poll the `tracked-controls` gamepad axes on `#rhand`/`#lhand`, nudge `state.a/.b`, and call the existing `recompute()`.*

### Next (weeks)

- **A comfort and access panel before you enter.** Snap-turn, a vignette option, a seated and one-controller mode, and — the one nobody builds — a table-height control: `#table` sits at a fixed `y=0.92`, which is chest height on a nine-year-old standing in `local-floor`. *First step: add a height-and-comfort row to `buildPresets()` in vr.js that writes `position` on `#table`, `#console` and `#presets` in vr.html, and persist it in `localStorage`.*
- **Grab a thread and read it; pull it and break it.** The tubes in `threadGroup` aren't raycastable at all today — only the instanced pins are. Make links pickable, show `whyOf()` on pinch, and let a hard pull snap the link so every downstream station goes dark in front of you. *First step: change `findChains(state, limit)` in threads.js to `findChains(state, limit, banned = new Set())` with one `banned.has(nx.id)` guard — the flat atlas gets the same counterfactual for free.*
- **Confidence you can see.** Render `ca:true` events and debated links as hazier, thinner, softer-edged tubes than settled ones, so "we are guessing" reads as a material property in a medium where solidity is believable. *First step: branch on `e.ca` in `buildPins()`/`drawThreads()` in vr.js for opacity and radius, before any new fields land in data.js.*
- **Captions on everything spoken.** `narrate()` is speech-only; a classroom is loud, Quest speakers leak, and deaf students get nothing. *First step: have `narrate()` in vr.js also push its text into the in-scene HUD plane, wrapped by the existing `wrap()` helper.*
- **A spectator view for the projector.** Quest casting mirrors the wearer's eye, which is nauseating for thirty watching kids; render a stable third-person camera on the corridor with large captions instead. *First step: add a second `<a-entity camera>` in vr.html outside `#rig` and a `spectator` toggle in vr.js that points it at the current station.*

### Later (months)

- **Two in the room, no cloud, no accounts.** A tiny opt-in relay the teacher runs next to the `python3 -m http.server` the README already documents: pages join only with `?room=`, and without it nothing changes. Presence is a pointer beam and a first name — no audio, no avatars, no persistence. *First step: write `tools/room.mjs` as a dependency-free node SSE relay broadcasting `{a,b,pick,step}`, and have `recompute()` in vr.js publish to it when `?room=` is present.*
- **The atlas as a time tower.** Once spacing-by-years proves itself, lift the 189 pins off the table plane by year and let a kid walk up through the centuries instead of across a map. *First step: prototype a `y = toPos(e.y) * H` offset in `buildPins()` in vr.js behind a `?tower=1` flag.*
- **Spatial narration.** Per-station positional audio in the corridor, so you hear the next event ahead of you and the one you left behind you. *First step: attach a `THREE.PositionalAudio` per card in `buildCorridor()` in vr.js, fed by the same text `narrate()` already builds.*

### Not doing

- **Extruding or decimating land.js for the GPU.** The table is a single canvas texture built once in `mapCanvas()` — one draw call. Turning 91 KB of coastline into geometry buys detail nobody leans in to see and spends the entire frame budget to do it.
- **Any locomotion at all** — teleport arcs, stick-walking, flying over a globe. Snapping station to station is the reason kids can use this for twenty minutes; refusing locomotion is a feature, not a gap.
- **Photoreal period reconstructions** (standing in Gutenberg's workshop, a Han papermaking yard). Every modeled detail is an unsourced truth claim, and an asset pipeline ends the double-click-a-file ethos in one commit.

## Content, platform & reach track

The atlas already ships more platform than most solo projects ever get to: CI runs check.mjs, apcheck.mjs and a real headless-browser smoke.mjs on every push, Pages deploys it over HTTPS, `tools/make-pages.mjs` generates 189 readable event pages plus sitemap.xml, and every view has a URL. The bet for this track is that the next unit of growth comes not from new features but from **who can get in and who can trust it**: a blind kid can't currently reach a single event, 66 of 189 events carry `ca:true` that nothing renders, the chain view hides the other causes of 52 events that have more than one, and the page still makes exactly one third-party request (Google Fonts) that an IT reviewer will grep for. Fix the on-ramps and the honesty, then open authoring so the dataset can outgrow one maintainer.

### Now (days)

- **A way in that isn't a 4.2px circle.** Today the only entry point is one of 189 `<circle class="ev" tabindex="0">` with no `aria-label` (app.js:45), so a screen reader says "clickable" 189 times and a keyboard user tabs forever. Add a combobox above the map populated from `EVENTS`, calling the existing `openEvent(id)`, plus an `aria-label` on every dot. *First step: add `<input id="eventSearch" role="combobox">` and a `<ul role="listbox">` into the `.stage` section of index.html, and in app.js set the circle's `aria-label` to the event's title, date and place in the `el("circle", …)` call at line 45.*
- **Stop the panel re-reading itself.** `#panel` is `aria-live="polite"` (index.html:186) and `renderBridge`/`renderRipple` replace its entire `innerHTML` on every Reveal, Next link and tab click — the whole chain gets re-announced each time, which makes class mode and hide-the-middle unusable non-visually. *First step: add a visually-hidden `<div id="srStatus" aria-live="polite" aria-atomic="true">` to index.html, set `#panel` to `aria-live="off"`, and write one line into it from the `classBtn`/`nextBtn`/`prevBtn`/`data-reveal` handlers in app.js:183-187.*
- **Show the guesses.** A third of the atlas (66 events) is `ca:true` and nothing on screen says so beyond a "c." prefix. A "Show me the guesses" toggle that recolours every `ca` dot and its arcs amber turns the whole deep-past half of the map over at once — the fastest lesson in "before writing, history is reconstruction" the app can give. *First step: add a `.ev.guess` dotted-amber rule to the `<style>` block in index.html and a toggle in `.presets` that sets the class from `byId.get(c.dataset.id).ca` inside `render()` in app.js:124.*
- **"+N other causes" on every chain link.** 52 events have more than one incoming link (one has six), but `renderBridge` draws the single edge that happens to lie on the traced path — which is exactly the single-thread-of-destiny story the app should be undercutting. *First step: add `otherCauses(to, usedFrom) => inn.get(to).filter(l => l.from !== usedFrom)` beside `whyOf` in threads.js and splice a badge into the `.why` paragraph template at app.js:178.*
- **Zero third-party requests, provably.** index.html:36-39 is the only outbound call in the whole app. Self-host the three woff2 files, then write `check-privacy.mjs` that greps the pages for `fetch`, `cookie` and external `<script src>` and fails CI, and a `trust.html` one-pager stating what a district reviewer asks. *First step: download the three faces into `fonts/`, add `@font-face` blocks to theme.css, delete the two `<link>` tags and `<noscript>` in index.html, and add `node check-privacy.mjs` as a step in `.github/workflows/ci.yml`.*
- **Make the gaps a number.** By lat/lon bucket the atlas is Europe 63, N. America 46, Asia 37, Africa/MENA 26, S. America 9, S/SE Asia 3, Oceania 2. Nobody can fix what nobody prints. *First step: write `coverage.mjs` loading data.js the way check.mjs:3 does, print a region × century × kind table, and add it as a non-failing CI step.*

### Next (weeks)

- **Sources and debate as first-class fields.** `ev()` already spreads `...o`, so `{src: [...], debate: "..."}` is a non-breaking addition. Backfill the 66 `ca:true` events first — those are what a skeptical teacher asks about — and render both in the panel and on the generated `e/` pages. Extend check.mjs to require `src` for any id outside a hardcoded `CORE_IDS` allowlist, so growth is sourced even though the original 189 aren't yet. *First step: extend the header comment and `ev()` call at data.js:10, populate `src`/`debate` for `maize`, `farming`, `buddha`, `bantu`, `australia`, then render it in `renderRipple` (app.js:209) and in `tools/make-pages.mjs`.*
- **Actually offline, not just installable.** site.webmanifest exists but there is no service worker, so a Chromebook cart with no wifi gets nothing — and vr.html pulls A-Frame from a CDN. One `sw.js` covers all three entry points, including the exam kit's three-hour run. *First step: write `sw.js` precaching the static files, register it from index.html, ap.html and vr.html, vendor `aframe-master.min.js` next to the page (CI already downloads it for smoke.mjs), and add an offline assertion to smoke.mjs.*
- **`contribute.html`: authoring without git.** The only way to add history today is hand-editing a 422-line file. A static page that loads data.js, lets someone click the map for lat/lon, drag the year, pick a kind, and emits a copy-pasteable `ev(...)`/`ln(...)` line — validated in-browser by the same code check.mjs runs — is the unlock from 189 events to thousands. *First step: extract the id/time/kind checks from check.mjs:5-13 into `validate.js` callable from both node and the browser, and prove `node check.mjs` still passes unchanged.*
- **Fill the thin regions, steered by `coverage.mjs`.** Oceania (2), South and Southeast Asia (3) and South America (9) aren't editorial taste, they're a measurable hole. Pair it with an `{oral: true}` flag and a badge for events like `australia` and `bantu` that reach us through memory rather than writing, so absence of text doesn't read as absence of history. *First step: add `oral` to the `ev()` options in data.js, tag `australia` and `bantu`, add a badge in app.js and `tools/make-pages.mjs`, then add 20 events against the emptiest `coverage.mjs` buckets.*
- **Paper and LMS reach.** theme.css has one throwaway `@media print` rule. Turn the current chain into a printable numbered worksheet (with the hidden steps as blanks and an answer key after a page break), and add a Google Classroom share chip beside `#copyLink` reusing the URL `writeURL()` already maintains. *First step: add a `#printChain` container populated in `render()` (app.js:122) and a real `@media print` block in theme.css beside the existing `.sitenav` rule.*

### Later (months)

- **Localization as a data layer, not a fork.** Every sentence lives in one `s` field; add `s_es`, `s_fr` (or an `i18n/es.js` overlay keyed by id) plus a `?lang=` parameter, and let translations arrive through the same PR path as history. `tools/make-pages.mjs` then emits per-language `e/` pages with `hreflang`, which is where non-English search traffic actually comes from. *First step: add a `lang` lookup in threads.js beside `when()` that falls back to `e.s`, and an `i18n/es.js` covering the 30 events on the README's headline chain.*
- **Two grandmothers: a second framing on contested events.** An optional `s2` plus credit on 15–20 flashpoint events (1492, conquest, colonial trade), with a flip toggle on the card. This is months, not weeks, because it needs real sourcing per event rather than a rewrite pass. *First step: add `s2`/`src2` to the `ev()` options in data.js, write it for `columbus` and two conquest events, and add the toggle in `renderRipple`'s card template (app.js:209).*
- **Provenance on links: strength, sources, counter-argument.** Only 2 of 209 `why` strings currently hedge, yet the README claims several do. Extend `ln(from, to, why, {strength, sources, counter})`, render contested links dashed, and make check.mjs flag anything marked `strong` with no counter-argument on file. *First step: add the optional fourth argument to `ln()` at data.js:11 with safe defaults, backfill the twenty links on the paper→Moon chain, and branch on it in `drawThread` (app.js:36).*
- **A syllabus lens for other exam boards.** A hand-tagged `syllabus.js` mapping event ids to IB, GCSE and state-standard codes, driving a "dim what isn't on my spec" filter — the honest version of curriculum alignment, and the content half of the AP track's `curricula.js`. *First step: create `syllabus.js` with a `tag(id, codes[])` helper, tag 30 events against one real spec, and wire a single dropdown in app.js that drops untagged nodes to low opacity.*

### Not doing

- **Contributor bylines in data.js** ("added by Maya, 11, Toledo") — publishing a child's name, age and town as permanent public repo data is precisely the PII the rest of this track is working to eliminate; credit belongs in commit history, not in the shipped dataset.
- **Paste-your-syllabus fuzzy coverage percentage** — substring matching exam-board prose against 189 titles will confidently report "73% covered" to a teacher planning a unit, and a wrong number is worse than no number; the hand-tagged `syllabus.js` layer above is the honest, slower version.
- **Named-kid mastery tracking and streaks** — it needs a durable per-child identity to be useful, which invites real names into `localStorage` and turns a no-account tool into a thing that remembers children; per-session feedback inside one lesson gets most of the teaching value at none of the cost.

## How this document was made

- **80 ideation agents** worked 20 themes — accessibility, historiographical rigor, teacher workflow, VR craft on Quest, exam fidelity, authoring pipeline, offline and touch, audio, representation, privacy, and ten more — from 4 angles each (what's broken here, what a competitor does better, what only this codebase could do, what a kid would tell a friend about), producing 240 raw ideas against the actual source files.
- **16 scoring judges** rated every idea on impact, cost and fit with the project's constraints, cutting 240 to the 141 that survived; ideas that needed a build step, a server, an API key or a child's identity were dropped on fit regardless of how good they sounded.
- **4 synthesis agents** clustered the survivors into the three tracks above, sorted each into days / weeks / months, wrote a "not doing" list per track so the refusals are on the record, and this editing pass merged duplicates and resolved the places where two tracks proposed the same file.

*The ranking is editorial judgment from reading the code, not evidence from users — no kid or teacher has voted on any of it yet, and the first real classroom run should be allowed to reorder this list.*
